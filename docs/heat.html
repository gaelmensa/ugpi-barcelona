What It Measures

What this layer captures

The heat layer measures the Land Surface Temperature (LST) of every census tract in Barcelona during the summer months of June, July and August 2023. LST is derived from the thermal infrared band (B10) of Landsat satellite imagery. Dense urban surfaces like asphalt and concrete absorb and re-emit heat, creating Urban Heat Islands that can be up to 7 degrees Celsius hotter than surrounding areas. Tracts with high heat scores consistently show high proportions of impervious surface cover and low vegetation density.

Score Distribution

How tracts are ranked.

Raw LST values are normalized to a 1 to 10 scale using min-max normalization across all 1,068 census tracts. A score of 10 represents the hottest tract, a score of 1 represents the coolest.

Key Findings

What the data shows.

Finding 01

Sants-Montjuic is Barcelona's most heat-stressed district, driven by dense building fabric, extensive impervious surfaces and critically low vegetation cover across most of its census tracts.

Finding 02

Coolest areas: Tracts adjacent to Collserola park and the coast score below 3, confirming the cooling effect of large green spaces and proximity to the sea.

Finding 03

Temperature gap: A 7 degree Celsius difference exists between the hottest and coolest tracts, demonstrating severe intra-city inequality in heat exposure.
