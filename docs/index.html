The Challenge

Barcelona is getting hotter. Not everyone feels it equally.

Who

Elderly residents, children and low-income families in dense neighborhoods face the greatest health risk from extreme heat.

What

Urban Heat Islands create temperature differences of up to 7 degrees Celsius between neighborhoods just kilometers apart.

Where

Dense central districts like Ciutat Vella and Eixample have up to 80 percent less green space per capita than outer districts.

When

Barcelona recorded its hottest summers on record in 2022 and 2023, a trend that will intensify with climate change.

What Makes It New

The first tool to combine all three.

Existing Barcelona planning tools map heat, green space or social vulnerability in isolation. The UGPI is the first to formally integrate all three into a single composite score designed as an equitable planning directive.

Heat

Land Surface Temperature from Landsat at 30m

Previously only accessible to GIS specialists. Now surfaced as an actionable score for every census tract.

Explore the data
Green Space

NDVI vegetation index from Sentinel-2 at 10m

Inverted so low vegetation equals high deficit. Tells planners not just where green space exists, but where it is missing most.

Explore the data
Social Vulnerability

Elderly population share plus low income index

The only existing Barcelona index to embed equity as a formal input. A vulnerable tract is structurally prioritised, not just noted.
