About the Project

A student team.
A real problem.

UGPI Barcelona was built by five ESADE students as part of the Perspectives on AI, Business and Data programme. Every number in the tool comes from real satellite imagery and real census data.

Project Context

What this project is and why it exists.

The Urban Greening Priority Index is a group project developed within the ESADE Business School Perspectives on AI, Business and Data (PAIBS) programme during the 2025-2026 academic year. The project brief was to apply AI, machine learning and data science to a real-world business or societal problem.

We chose urban heat inequality in Barcelona because it is measurable, geographically concrete, and directly connected to a real policy gap: the Ajuntament de Barcelona has access to the underlying data but no integrated tool to translate it into equitable investment decisions.

The project combines three fully independent data streams: thermal infrared satellite imagery, multispectral vegetation imagery, and municipal census data into a single composite score for every one of Barcelona's 1,068 census tracts. All data sources are free, open and reproducible. The full analysis pipeline is written in Python and runs in hours on a standard laptop.

Research Question

The question we set out to answer.

"What forces account for the misallocation of urban greening investments, which leaves the most vulnerable communities disproportionately exposed to the dangers of extreme heat?"

UGPI Barcelona, ESADE PAIBS Group 8, 2025-2026

Our guiding star is a system for intelligent urban climate adaptation that produces healthier, more equitable, and climate-resilient cities. The UGPI is not a research exercise; it is designed to be a decision support tool that city planners can open in a browser, explore in minutes, and use to justify real budget allocations.

Project Timeline

How we built it.

Sep 2025

Problem scoping and literature review

Identified Barcelona urban heat inequality as the focus. Reviewed urban heat island research, existing Barcelona environmental data sources, and state-of-the-art EO methods for urban vegetation analysis.

Completed
Nov 2025

Three stakeholder interviews

Conducted three independent interviews: Davoud Omarzadeh (Research Manager, IEEC) validated the technical concept and confirmed the data integration gap; Rosa Tabeni (retired Eixample resident) provided lived testimony on daily heat exposure and mobility barriers; Dr. Jose Zorrilla (Emergency Physician, Althaia Hospital) confirmed clinical patterns of heat-related harm and validated the dual-axis prioritisation logic.

Completed
Dec 2025 to Feb 2026

Data pipeline construction

Built Python scripts to download Sentinel-2 and Landsat imagery via the Copernicus API, calculate NDVI and LST at 10m and 30m resolution, process Idescat census data, and merge all three layers into the UGPI score at census tract level.

Completed
Mar 2026

Interactive map tool

Built the Folium-based interactive HTML map with choropleth layers, adjustable weight sliders, priority filter, top-20 panel, district view, compare tracts mode, intervention simulator and CSV export.

Completed
Apr to May 2026

Website and final presentation

Built the UGPI website with methodology documentation, business validation analysis and interactive map. Preparing the final group presentation and written report for May 2026 submission.

In progress
