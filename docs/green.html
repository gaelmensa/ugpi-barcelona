What It Measures

Measuring vegetation from space

The green space deficit layer measures the density of vegetation in every census tract in Barcelona during summer 2023. NDVI (Normalised Difference Vegetation Index) is calculated from Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery using the near-infrared (B8) and red (B4) bands at 10 metre resolution. NDVI values range from -1 to +1, where values above 0.2 indicate meaningful vegetation and values near 0 indicate bare urban surfaces such as asphalt and concrete. The score is then inverted so that low vegetation equals a high deficit score, meaning tracts with the least green space receive the highest priority signal.

Score Distribution

How tracts are ranked.

NDVI values are inverted and normalized to a 1 to 10 scale across all 1,068 census tracts. A score of 10 represents the highest green space deficit (lowest vegetation). Most Barcelona tracts score above 5, reflecting the city's predominantly urban built fabric.

Key Findings

What the data shows.

Finding 01

Barcelona's mean NDVI of 0.21 is critically low. Dense districts like Eixample and Gracia average below 0.15, indicating surfaces that are almost entirely impervious with negligible cooling vegetation.

Finding 02

The vegetation gap between the greenest and sparsest tracts spans from 0.57 to 0.06 NDVI, a nearly 10x difference. Tracts near Collserola park benefit from a natural buffer unavailable to central neighborhoods.

Finding 03

Over 56 tracts score 9 or above on the green deficit scale, concentrated in Nou Barris, Sants-Montjuic and Ciutat Vella - the same districts that also score highest on heat and social vulnerability.

Data Source

Where the data comes from.

Satellite Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A
Bands used B8 Near-Infrared + B4 Red
Formula NDVI = (B8 - B4) / (B8 + B4)
Resolution 10 m, zonal mean per census tract
Period June, July, August 2023 (least cloud cover)
Cloud masking SCL band, pixels with cloud flag excluded
