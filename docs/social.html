What It Measures

Who is most exposed to heat risk

Not all residents face the same risk from rising temperatures. The social vulnerability layer identifies which census tracts concentrate the populations least able to protect themselves from extreme heat: the elderly, who are physiologically more sensitive to heat stress, and low-income households, who cannot afford air conditioning or temporary relocation during heat waves. Two sub-indicators are independently normalised to a 1 to 10 scale and averaged into a composite vulnerability score. A high score signals that a tract combines a large proportion of older residents with below-average income, creating compounded exposure to climate risk.

Score Distribution

How tracts are ranked.

The composite vulnerability score runs from 2.8 to 8.3 across all 1,068 Barcelona census tracts, with a mean of 5.8. Scores cluster in the upper half of the scale, reflecting a city where social vulnerability is broadly distributed rather than isolated to a few areas.

Key Findings

What the data shows.

Finding 01

Some tracts in Nou Barris reach 41% elderly population share - meaning more than 2 in 5 residents are aged 65 or above. These tracts face concentrated heat risk with minimal private coping capacity.

Finding 02

The correlation between low income and high elderly share is strongest in Nou Barris and Sants-Montjuic, reinforcing the equity argument: neighborhoods that need the most investment have historically received the least.

Finding 03

Social vulnerability is absent from all existing Barcelona greening investment tools. The UGPI is the first index to formally embed it alongside environmental data, ensuring equity is structurally built into priority decisions.
