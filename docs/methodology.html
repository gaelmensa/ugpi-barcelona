Methodology

How we measure
greening priority.

The UGPI score combines three independent satellite and census data streams into a single normalized index for every census tract in Barcelona.

Summer 2023 Analysis
1,068 Census Tracts
The Index
UGPI = w₁ · Heat + w₂ · Green + w₃ · Social
Heat 33% Green 33% Social 34%

Each of the three component scores is independently derived from a different data source and normalized to a 1–10 scale, where 10 represents the highest priority. The final UGPI score is the weighted mean of the three components. By default all weights are equal at 33% each, reflecting a balanced approach to heat resilience, green space equity and social protection. However, weights are fully adjustable in the interactive map, allowing planners to reflect their specific policy priorities. A tract scoring 9 or above on the combined index represents a critical intervention zone where heat stress, lack of vegetation and social vulnerability all converge.

Limitations

What this tool does not do.

Transparency about limitations is part of responsible data science. The UGPI is a planning decision support tool, not a predictive model. Users should understand its assumptions before drawing conclusions.

Data period

All satellite data is from summer 2023, the most recent complete season available at time of analysis. Scores do not reflect very recent greening interventions or extreme weather events after August 2023. The index should be refreshed annually with each new summer satellite season.

LST vs air temperature

Land Surface Temperature (LST) measures the thermal emission of ground surfaces, not the air temperature experienced by residents. LST is a reliable proxy for Urban Heat Island intensity but should not be read as a direct measure of felt heat stress, which also depends on wind, humidity and shade.

Social indicators

The social vulnerability composite uses two indicators: elderly share and income. Other relevant factors -- disability, immigration status, housing type, social isolation -- are not included due to data availability at census tract level. A richer social index would require access to administrative micro-data.

Equal weight default

The default equal weighting (33/33/34) is a deliberate neutral starting point, not a validated policy prescription. The interactive tool exists precisely so that planners can override defaults with weights that reflect their specific mandate. No single weighting is objectively correct.
