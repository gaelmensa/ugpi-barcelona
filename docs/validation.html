Technical Expert Interview

Validated by a domain expert.

In November 2024 we interviewed Davoud Omarzadeh, Research Manager at the Institut d'Estudis Espacials de Catalunya (IEEC), recommended by Professor Marc Herrera. He validated the core concept and the technical approach, and shaped key design decisions in the UGPI.

"

Combining Earth Observation data with social vulnerability indicators is exactly the kind of integrated approach that urban planning needs. City planners have had access to massive amounts of open data since 2020, but the bottleneck is synthesis, not data.

Davoud Omarzadeh

Research Manager, IEEC, Institut d'Estudis Espacials de Catalunya

Interview conducted November 2024

Key takeaways from the interview

The data overload problem is real

Planners are overwhelmed by siloed datasets from different departments. The UGPI's single composite score directly addresses this bottleneck by synthesising three independent data streams into one actionable output.

BCN Geoportal confirms the gap

He confirmed that while the BCN Geoportal and ICGC are authoritative data sources, no existing tool integrates heat, vegetation and social data into a single priority index for planning decisions.

Next validation step: Ajuntament de Barcelona

He recommended presenting a complete tool proposal to the Ajuntament de Barcelona urban planning department and ASPB before approaching them for user testing, which shaped our build-first approach.

User and Community Research

Validated by those who feel it daily.

Beyond the technical expert, we interviewed a community resident and a frontline health professional. Both independently confirmed that the problem the UGPI addresses is not theoretical.

"

"The street, instead of being a place to walk, feels like an enemy. And we have no shade, no trees, nothing to protect us from the heat."

"They are expelling us, the older people, from the city. It used to be ours too."

Rosa Tabeni

Retired resident, Eixample district

Interview conducted November 2024

Key insight

As a 72-year-old resident of Eixample, one of Barcelona's densest and hottest districts, Rosa described summer heat as a daily barrier to mobility and social life. Her account directly validates the social vulnerability layer: elderly residents in low-canopy areas face disproportionate harm that the UGPI is designed to surface and prioritise.

"

"From a health point of view, the best strategy is to target areas where you have both high exposure and high vulnerability. Those are the places where a single intervention saves the most lives."

Dr. Jose Zorrilla

Emergency Medicine Physician, Althaia Hospital, Manresa

Interview conducted November 2024

Key insight

Dr. Zorrilla confirmed that heat-related emergency presentations peak in dense urban neighbourhoods with older populations and described the clinical burden of heat stress on the healthcare system. His framing of "high exposure and high vulnerability" is the precise logic embedded in the UGPI composite score, independently validating the dual-axis prioritisation approach.

Problem Validation

A real problem, confirmed by data.

The UGPI addresses a documented planning failure: resources are not systematically allocated to the neighbourhoods most exposed to heat stress and least equipped to cope with it.

The Heat Gap

A 7 degree Celsius divide within the same city

Land Surface Temperature analysis across 1,068 Barcelona census tracts shows a 7 degree Celsius difference between the coolest and hottest tracts. Dense urban cores in Sants-Montjuic and Nou Barris consistently register the highest temperatures, while park-adjacent or coastal tracts remain significantly cooler.

The Vegetation Deficit

Barcelona's mean NDVI is critically low at 0.21

Sentinel-2 satellite imagery shows Barcelona's average vegetation index at 0.21, far below recommended thresholds for urban heat mitigation. The most built-up central tracts fall as low as 0.06, offering almost no evapotranspirative cooling to residents who live there.

The Equity Failure

The most vulnerable residents live in the hottest places

Social vulnerability scores from Idescat census data reveal that high elderly population share and below-average income are concentrated in the same districts that record the highest heat and lowest vegetation scores. This is not coincidental; it reflects decades of unequal investment in public green infrastructure.

The Decision Gap

Planners have data but no decision-ready synthesis

The Ajuntament de Barcelona and ASPB have access to all three data streams independently, but no integrated tool that translates them into a prioritised list of census tracts ready for budget allocation. The UGPI closes this gap with a single, transparent, adjustable score.

Value Proposition

One sentence. One tool.

For Barcelona city planners who need to allocate limited greening budgets equitably, the UGPI provides a single actionable priority score for every census tract, combining satellite heat data, vegetation indices and social vulnerability, unlike fragmented departmental data systems. It synthesises complex multi-source data into one decision-ready index that any planner can use without GIS expertise.

Business Model

Built to scale.

The UGPI is designed as a B2G (Business-to-Government) SaaS product. The city pays an annual subscription for updated analysis, maintained infrastructure and simulation features. The underlying data is entirely free and open, making the cost structure exceptionally lean.

Revenue model

B2G Annual Subscription

The Ajuntament de Barcelona pays a yearly fee for: annual data refresh using the latest summer satellite season, maintained interactive tool, simulation and export features, and onboarding support for planning staff. Long-term vision: licence the model to other Mediterranean cities (Valencia, Marseille, Palermo) using the same methodology with local data inputs.

Scalability

Any city with open EO data

The three data sources used are globally available via free APIs. Copernicus covers all of Europe; Landsat covers the world. Adapting the UGPI pipeline to a new city requires updating the census boundary file and re-running the same Python scripts. The web tool requires no changes.

Innovation

What makes UGPI genuinely new.

Existing tools map heat, green space or social vulnerability in isolation. The UGPI is the first tool to formally integrate all three into a single composite index designed as an equitable planning directive for municipal decision-makers in Barcelona.

Innovation 01

Three streams, one score

No existing Barcelona planning tool combines thermal EO, vegetation EO and social census data. UGPI is the first to fuse them into a single, transparent, weighted index at census tract granularity.

Innovation 02

Equity built into the index

Social vulnerability is not an afterthought. It is a formal input to the score. This means a high-heat low-income tract is structurally prioritised over a high-heat high-income tract, embedding equity directly into resource allocation.

Innovation 03

Planners set the policy weights

The adjustable weight system allows city departments to reflect their current policy priorities in real time. A public health focus increases the social weighting; a climate resilience mandate increases the heat weighting. The data does not change. The interpretation does.
